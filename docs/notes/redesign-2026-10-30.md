# Redesign, 2026-10-30: layout, clicks and hover

The user went through the app and listed what felt wrong. This note turns the list into rules and designs. The designs are in Paper, page "Redesign, Oct 30" of `01M6342D512B1CKH0D29HMDN8V`.

The Paper file was reorganized first: one page for each area (Chat, Tasks, Channels and sidebar, Settings, Mobile), a labeled row for each flow on every page, and a Superseded page for old versions. Earlier notes that say "page Desktop" mean the page that is now called Chat, or the page of the area.

## The rules

The UX term for what the user asked is predictability (also "the principle of least surprise"): every click does what the user expects, and the app never moves the user somewhere they did not ask to go. Five rules make that concrete:

1. **A click stays where you are, unless its label says where it goes.** A link that leaves the current place says so ("Show in channel") and has the `external-link` icon. Everything else opens in place, with a Back.
2. **The same kind of control does the same kind of thing everywhere.** Every button at the top right of the chat toggles a panel on the right. Every row that has a menu opens the same menu from its "..." button and from a right click.
3. **Nothing moves when you hover.** Space for controls that show on hover is always there. Hover changes color and opacity, never size.
4. **The way back is where you came from.** The button that opened a panel closes it. A drill-in has Back at the top left of the same place.
5. **Two ways in, one place.** Duplicate entry points are fine when they open the same UI. Two different UIs for one job are not.

## The items

### 1. Group headers grow on hover (bug)

The "+" and "..." buttons are taller than the header row, so the row grows 2 to 4 px when they show. Fix: the row has a fixed height (the sidebar group label row), and the two buttons sit in fixed slots that are always there; at rest they are transparent. The same for channel rows.

### 2. Right click on group headers and rows

A right click on a group header opens the same menu as its "..." button. The same for channel rows and every other row with a menu (rule 2). The "..." stays, because it shows that a menu exists.

### 3. The top of the chat is not part of the chat

The zone from the top of the window to the first item at rest (the mark, the activity pill and the top actions, `layout.content_top`) is part of the window, not of the timeline:
- It takes all pointer events: no text selection starts there and nothing under it gets a click.
- The cursor is the arrow, not the text cursor.
- It is a window drag area, as a title bar is: drag to move the window, double click to zoom (macOS).
- The mark, the pill and the top actions keep their own cursors and clicks.

### 4. The top actions

Today: Connectors (opens a dialog), Channel `#` (toggles the Channel panel), Tasks (toggles the Tasks panel) and New task (a copy of Claude Duet's Make, which we do not need). Two of them open panels and one opens a dialog, so the same row of buttons does different things (rule 2).

New:
- **Two toggles: Channel and Tasks.** Each toggles its panel on the right. The open one has the selected ground. Tasks keeps its count badge.
- **New task goes.** The Tasks panel has "+" for a new task (rule 5: one place).
- **Connectors goes into the Channel panel as a tab:** Files, Routines, Memory, Connectors. The tab lists the connections and skills of this channel with their switches, and has Add a connector (the existing Add connector dialog) and "Manage all in Settings". The Connectors and skills dialog of a channel is replaced by the tab. In Tutti, the tab shows what Tutti can use.
- **Channel settings** stays a dialog, because it edits and saves. It opens from the gear in the Channel panel header and from Channel settings in the row menu (rule 5).

### 5. The left sidebar collapses, with no rail

- The Search row leaves the sidebar list. Search and Hide sidebar become two icon buttons on the traffic lights' center line, at the right end of the sidebar's title bar. Tooltips: "Search (⌘K)", "Hide sidebar (⌘\\)".
- Hide sidebar removes the sidebar. No rail, no icons. The chat takes the width. The traffic lights stay, and both buttons stay in the same order. The toggle is now Show sidebar.
- The two buttons slide as one group: left to sit next to the traffic lights when the sidebar closes, and right to the sidebar's right end when it opens. The slide has the same duration and easing as the sidebar, so the buttons stay in step with its edge. With reduced motion on, they move without the slide.
- ⌘\\ toggles it. ⌘K still searches with the sidebar hidden.
- The app remembers the choice.

### 6. The right panel resizes

- Drag the left edge of the panel. The hit area is 8 px wide, the line is 1 px, and on hover the line turns `border.strong` and the cursor is the column resize cursor. Double click resets the width.
- Least width 320 px. Default 400 px. The task item view starts at 560 px or the user's width, whichever is wider.
- The chat column never gets narrower than 480 px. When the window shrinks: first the panel shrinks to its least width, then the sidebar hides on its own. From 800 px down, chat and panel do not fit side by side, so the panel covers the window with Back, as in the compact layout. Under 760 px the window has the compact layout (item 7). When the window has no room for the sidebar, Show sidebar opens it as a drawer.
- The app remembers the width.

### 7. The compact layout

When the window is narrower than 760 px, the app becomes a single column, as Claude Duet does:
- Top bar: Menu (three lines) at the left opens the sidebar as a drawer over the chat with a scrim. The mark is in the middle with the channel name in a pill under it ("# sayso"); a click on the pill opens the Channel panel. At the right: the account avatar (opens Settings) and Tasks with its count badge.
- A panel (Channel, Tasks, a task thread) opens over the whole window with Back at the top left.
- Dialogs are full width with the page margin.
- Settings shows its list of sections, or one section with Back to the list. The least window width is now 420 by 560 px.
- When the window grows past 760 px, the normal layout comes back with the same channel and panel open.

### 8. A footer under messages, with space kept

Every message group of the assistant and of the user keeps one footer line under its last bubble (24 px), always. At rest the line is empty. On hover over the group it shows:
- **On every message:** three icon buttons (24 px, 16 px icon, `text.tertiary`): React, Reply in thread, Copy, with tooltips and no text labels, then the time in Label size. On a user group the row is aligned right and mirrored, so the icons stay at the bubble's edge.
- **On a proactive message only:** Useful and Too noisy as a bordered pair of text buttons at the right edge of the bubble, apart from the icons. They are not icons because no icon says "too noisy" clearly.

Because the line is always there, hover never moves the timeline (rule 3). The footer replaces today's Useful and Too noisy row that appears on hover and pushes the next message down. A Useful or Too noisy choice stays visible at rest as a single chip in the same place, so the user can see what they told the assistant. A click on the chip clears the choice.

React uses the new hand-drawn `smile` icon, Reply in thread uses `message` and Copy uses `copy`. A proactive message is a group of its own, so each one keeps its own Useful and Too noisy. The pair stays at the right edge of the 560 px bubble width, also when the bubble is shorter, so it has one fixed place.

### 10. Errors: in chat or in the corner

The user asked for two ways to show an error. The rule for which one:

- **In chat:** the assistant could not do what the user asked in this conversation. Rate limit, expired sign-in, a worker that restarted during a turn, an unknown failure of a turn, and a message that the hub refused.
- **In the corner:** something the user did in the app failed, and it has no place in the conversation. Save, rename, upload, or a hub that did not answer. Short information uses the corner too.

Errors under a form field and the offline pill stay as they are.

**The in-chat error** is a card in the assistant lane at the 560 px bubble width, on `surface.card`, with a 1 px border of `status.failed` at 40 percent and the tail corner at the bottom left. It has the `failed` icon, a title in Body semibold, and text in Body `text.secondary` that says what happened and what to do next. Under them are the actions, and Details at the right end of their row. Details opens the raw text of the harness as selectable mono text. The footer line under the card keeps its time. When the user tries again or sends another message, the card becomes one quiet line with the icon, the title and a chevron, and a click opens it. Old buttons do not stay in the history. The writing bubble does not show with the card.

| Kind | Title | Actions |
|---|---|---|
| Rate limit | "Claude Work reached its weekly limit" | Choose another model, Try again |
| Expired sign-in | "Claude Work needs a new sign-in" | Sign in again, Try again |
| Worker restarted | "The worker restarted during this turn" | Try again |
| Unknown | "The assistant stopped with an error" | Try again |

**A message that the hub refused** stays in the lane of the user, dimmed, with the reason under it and "Put back in the message box". Today this is a toast.

**The corner notification** is 360 px wide, at the right with a 16 px margin, on `surface.raised` with `shadow.float`. It sits above the composer (124 px from the bottom), and not in the corner itself, because in a 1440 px window the corner would cover Send. An error has the `failed` icon, a title in UI semibold, text in UI `text.secondary`, an optional action, and stays until the user dismisses it. Information is one line and leaves after 6 seconds. Three show at most, with the newest at the bottom. The others wait, as errors wait today. In the compact layout it takes the full width with a 16 px margin and sits under the top bar (88 px from the top), because above the composer it would cover the newest message and the actions of an in-chat error.

**What the build needs:** the harness error reaches the hub as one string. The worker must send the error kind and the reset time as fields, and the `turn_failed` notice must carry them to the client, so the app can write the title and choose the actions. A failure with no known kind uses the Unknown row.

### 11. Approvals, tasks and artifacts

The user compared Consort with Claude Duet on one request. Duet started a task without being told, ran on its own, and showed a document. Consort asked for many approvals in the chat and then posted a long answer as many messages. The aim: the chat stays clean.

**Approvals have two views.**

- **While it waits:** a card at the 560 px bubble width with the accent border. The title says what the action is, and the block under it says the exact command or target. The action shows once: when the summary repeats the command, the title falls back to "Run a command". A long command stops at 5 lines with Show all. The folder stays when the agent can choose it. All text wraps inside the box. The buttons are Allow once, Allow for this task and Deny, in one row of equal widths. A label over 20 characters does not fit that row: it gets its own row under the others, at full width, and wraps inside its button. A card with no task scope has no Allow for this task.
- **After the answer:** one line of 36 px in a box: a status glyph, the action cut to one line, the answer ("Allowed once", "Allowed for this task", "Denied", "Closed, no answer") and a chevron. A click opens the box in place with the full command and the folder. Nothing else in the timeline moves.

**Auto asks only when it must.** Today Auto on Claude Code runs as `acceptEdits`, so every command and web search asks. New rule:

- Auto maps to Claude Code's own `auto` mode. It asks for destructive actions and for actions outside the workspace. This changes DECISIONS 206, which blocked that mode as an escalation.
- New channels and Tutti default to Auto. An existing channel keeps its mode.
- Connector rules do not change: sending an email and deleting data still ask, by the connection's rules.
- Codex keeps its mapping. Gemini CLI and OpenCode have no equal mode, so they still ask before commands, and the mode's help text says so.
- Proactive turns still run in Ask.

**Work goes to a task.** The coordinator starts a task for any request that needs a shell, the web, file edits or more than a quick look, and the user does not need to ask for it. It answers directly only from what it knows, from memory, from the timeline, or with a single connector read. The prompt alone is not reliable, so the hub also enforces this: a coordinator session denies shell and web tools with a message that says "start a task". The chat gets one short message and the task line. A task thread shows its plan as a checklist, from the harness's plan items, under the newest message and above the writing bubble. The thread header shows the current step.

**Two ways to reply, one task.** The user can reply in the task thread or in the main chat.

- **In the thread:** this works today. The reply goes straight to the task session as a turn, a steer or an inbox message, and the coordinator does not wake.
- **In the main chat:** the coordinator decides whether the message is about an open task. If it is, the coordinator calls `message_task` and does not start a second task. It then posts one short line that says what happens next. If two open tasks could match, it asks which one. If none matches, it handles the message as usual. The coordinator does not reopen a Resolved task on its own, because only the user moves a task out of Resolved (with Not done). Related work starts a new task that names the old one.
- **The task gets the user's words.** `message_task` gets an optional `user_message_id`. The hub checks that the ID is a message from the user in this channel, sends its body to the task as a message from the user, and shows it in the thread as the user's bubble with Show in channel under it. Today the thread shows only the coordinator's own text as an assistant message. Other coordinator instructions stay assistant messages, and text from a web page or a task can never carry this ID's trust, because the hub reads the body from the stored message.
- **The chat knows what is open.** Today the list of open tasks is only in the `<context>` block of a session's first turn. Every turn with a user message now also gets the short list of open tasks: ID, title and status.
- **Questions reach the chat.** On `needs_input`, the coordinator posts the task's actual question in one bubble under the task line, in place of "a decision waits". A plain text answer in the chat goes to the task through `message_task`. An approval is never answered from the chat by text: the coordinator points to its card. A decision with options closes when the user's text answers it, so it does not stay open after the task moves on.

**The task thread has the same composer as the chat.** It is one component: the rounded field with Send inside, and under it add, voice, the model and effort chips, and where the task runs. Top padding, bottom padding and heights are the same, so with a panel open the two fields and the two tool rows line up at the bottom of the window. It replaces the plain reply box and the "Runs with" line. In a narrow panel the tool row drops "Runs on" first and then the account name.

A task can change its model after it started. Today the hub refuses this (`task.update`: "You can change only its title now").

- **The same account:** a new model or effort applies from the next turn, in the same session, so the task keeps its context. A running turn is not cut.
- **Another account or provider:** that is a different harness login, so the session cannot go on. The picker says "This starts a new session in the same folder." At the next turn the hub starts a new session with the task's folder and sends it the brief and the thread as context.
- If the harness cannot change model in a session, it is handled as another account.
- The task's selection is saved, so a retry uses it too.

**Long output is a document.** A task writes a long result as a Markdown artifact. When the task finishes, the coordinator posts one short message with the artifact attached. A click on the artifact card opens the document in the right panel, rendered, with Copy, Save and Open in another app in the header and the close button at the right end (rule 4). A file type that the panel cannot show opens in the system app, as today.

**A reply is one bubble.** A reply with a heading or a list stays in one bubble. The prompt no longer asks for several short messages when the content is long.

### 12. Drafts: the text is yours to change

The user compared Consort's send approval with Claude Duet's draft box. Consort already has a draft card on connector send tools (`render_draft_card` in `ui/voice.rs`), with Edit, commitment flags and the tick, but it reads as an approval: a table of fields and "Allow". The new card reads as the message that goes out.

**The card** is at the 560 px bubble width, with the accent border because it is the user's turn. The title row has the `message` icon, "Draft:" and a title, and Copy and Edit icon buttons at the right. Under it are To (with the connection) and Subject as plain lines, then a line, then the body at Body size. At the bottom: the hint "Edit it here, or tell me what to change.", Not now and Send. The hub writes the title from the subject, or "Email to <name>" when there is none.

**Words:** Send replaces Allow, and Not now replaces Don't. Send is the permission, so the card has no Allow for this task. Connector cards that do not send text keep the approval words of item 11.

**Edit in place.** Edit turns the subject and body into fields inside the same card, with Cancel and Save. Nothing else in the timeline moves more than the text needs. The hub scans the saved text again, as it does today.

**The assistant edits it.** The user says what to change in the chat or the task thread. While a draft waits, the session is blocked in its tool call, so a steer would not reach it. So when a user message arrives for a session with a waiting draft, the hub ends the card as "Replaced by a new version" and returns the user's message as the tool result, with the text "The user sent this while your draft waited. If it asks for a change, write the draft again and call the tool. If not, answer it and call the tool again with the same text." The new card takes the place of the old one in the timeline. This needs no button. Edits that the user saved stay in the old version's text, which the agent gets in the same result, so they are not lost.

**Flags.** With commitment flags, the marked text is underlined, the note is under the body, and the tick "I checked the marked line" takes the place of the hint. Send stays off until the tick, as today. "In your voice" is in the title row. "Not how I write" stays as a quiet link while the voice is on.

**No connection.** When the assistant has no connection that can send it (a claim form, a phone call), a new hub tool `show_draft` (title, to, body) posts the same card with no accent border, because nothing waits. It has "Consort cannot send this one for you.", Edit and Copy, and it does not stop the turn.

**After the answer** it is one line of 36 px that opens in place, as in item 11: "Sent to <name>", "Not sent", or "Replaced by a new version". The open line shows the text that went out.

**When it shows.** The rules for when a connector tool asks do not change. A send that the connection's rule lets run without asking runs without a card, except while the voice is on (DECISIONS 668). The prompts tell the assistant to use a draft for text in the user's name and to never write it as a plain chat message when a send or `show_draft` fits.
